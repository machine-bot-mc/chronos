## Full script
Good morning. It's Friday, September twenty-fifth, and this is your Chronos digest. Today: international news. Three of the week's biggest stories: a lavish state visit in Washington, a defiant speech and a mass walkout at the United Nations, and a hate-motivated attack on a synagogue in Ontario that one wounded police officer kept from becoming far worse.

Story one. Trump rolls out the red carpet for Xi Jinping.

President Trump hosted Chinese leader Xi Jinping for a state visit at the White House this week, as reported by NBC News on September twenty-fourth. Trump personally met Xi at Joint Base Andrews in Maryland on Wednesday and walked him down a hundred-foot red carpet, something Trump has not done for any other visiting leader. Thursday brought a second red-carpet ceremony at the White House and a formal state dinner in the East Room. At the dinner, Trump said, quote: As the leaders of our two nations, President Xi and I both understand that we represent different systems. End quote. He also called the relationship, quote: a truly great friendship. End quote, dating back to twenty sixteen.

Xi said the two leaders have kept up a personal rapport, and announced China will send two giant pandas to the Atlanta zoo.

The warmth drew sharp pushback from Trump's own party. Republican senator Roger Wicker of Mississippi said that had the White House asked his advice, he would have suggested against inviting Xi at all, citing the Chinese Communist Party. Senator Lisa Murkowski of Alaska called the red-carpet treatment, quote: a little bit over the top. End quote. And senator John Kennedy of Louisiana was blunter still, saying Xi, quote: learned to lie before he learned to talk. End quote.

The visit also set off a press fight. Trump barred CNN, MS NOW, and Politico from White House grounds for the visit, and several other networks pulled their video-pool coverage in solidarity. A federal judge ordered access restored, but by then CNN and MS NOW reporters had already missed two hours of Xi's arrival.

Here's why it matters. Just two months ago, Trump was accusing China of hacking American elections. Intelligence officials also suggest China may have supplied Iran with shoulder-launched missiles and stealth-detecting radar during its war with Israel. And despite that, the administration agreed to extend a trade truce over Chinese rare-earth exports that was due to expire November the tenth, pushing it instead to January the tenth. Retired admiral Mark Montgomery summed up Trump's position at the summit this way, quote: We have a president entering this playing a weak hand. End quote, with the Iran war still dragging on and midterm elections that could cost Republicans their House majority.

Story two. Netanyahu defies a walkout at the United Nations.

Israeli prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu addressed the U N General Assembly in New York on September twenty-fourth, also reported by NBC News. As he began speaking, hundreds of diplomats walked out of the hall in protest over Israel's conduct in Gaza. Netanyahu responded, quote: If there are any more moral cowards who haven't left the hall, please do so now. End quote.

He defended the war with Iran, saying, quote: If we hadn't done it, we'd all be dead. End quote, and told the assembly, quote: We will continue to defend our state. We will continue to win because we have no other choice. End quote. He rejected accusations of genocide in Gaza outright, calling them, quote: the greatest lie of the century. End quote.

There are real numbers behind that dispute. Palestinian health officials say more than seventy-five thousand people have died in Israel's operations in Gaza. New York City's mayor, Zohran Mamdani, whom Netanyahu addressed directly during the speech, said afterward that the prime minister, quote: used his speech to repeat baseless lies meant to sanitize his genocide of Palestinians. End quote, and pointed to the International Criminal Court's arrest warrant against him. Netanyahu faces Israeli elections next month.

Story three. An officer's stand in Belleville, Ontario.

On Sunday evening, during Yom Kippur, a gunman opened fire outside the Sons of Jacob Synagogue in Belleville, Ontario, in what police call a deliberate, hate-motivated attack on the city's Jewish community, reported by CBS News in an update published September twenty-fourth. Belleville police chief Murray Rodd said, quote: This was a deliberate attack directed at members of the Jewish community during one of the holiest observances of the year. End quote.

Constable Jeff Smith was shot multiple times with a shotgun almost as soon as he arrived, before he could get out of his vehicle. Despite life-threatening wounds to his head and torso, he kept engaging the attacker for close to three minutes until a second officer, constable Mike McCurdy, arrived. The gunman, twenty-nine-year-old Sean Ward of Quinte West, died from injuries sustained in the exchange. No civilians were killed.

Ward had brought multiple firearms, a large supply of ammunition, and containers of gasoline. Chief Rodd said the two officers' actions prevented, quote: what would have become an unimaginable tragedy. End quote. Constable Smith remains hospitalized, facing more surgery for serious head trauma, but is, in the department's words, making progress every day.

Belleville police haven't released a specific ideology behind the attack beyond calling it hate-motivated and directed at the Jewish community on its holiest day. What's clear from what officers found in Ward's vehicle, the guns, the ammunition, and the gasoline, is that he came prepared for something far larger than the two officers who stopped him.

That's the shape of this week's world news: two leaders talking past their critics from stages an ocean apart, and one Canadian police officer who never got the chance to talk at all, only to act.

That's your digest. It's Friday, September twenty-fifth. Have a good one.
