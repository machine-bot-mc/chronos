Good morning. It's Monday, September twenty-eighth, and this is your Chronos digest. Today: professional sports. Baseball's regular season ended with the most improbable division title in the sport's history, the NFL just wrapped a wild third week with four teams still perfect, and golf's Presidents Cup delivered the biggest final-day comeback the event has ever seen.

Story one. Baseball's most improbable division winner.

The Houston Astros clinched the American League West on Sunday, the final day of the regular season, by beating the Athletics nine to nothing. That win locked in a record of eighty-one wins and eighty-one losses, exactly five hundred, which ESPN and Yahoo Sports both reported is the worst record ever posted by a division winner across a full one hundred sixty-two game season. The previous mark was the two thousand five San Diego Padres, who won their division at eighty-two and eighty. Houston finished one game ahead of Texas, who ended at eighty and eighty-two, and manager Joe Espada's team would have won the division even with a loss on the final day, since Houston held the tiebreaker. ESPN notes it's Houston's fifth AL West title in the last six years, just not one many saw coming from a team that spent stretches of the summer under five hundred.

Here's why it matters. A losing team is still going to the playoffs. Houston now hosts the Chicago White Sox in a best of three wild card series starting Tuesday. Elsewhere, the bracket is set. Tampa Bay, Cleveland and Milwaukee won their divisions outright, and Los Angeles and Atlanta took the National League West and East. The wild card round pairs the White Sox at Houston, Boston at New York, Philadelphia at Atlanta, and the Cubs at San Diego, all best of three, starting this week. Early odds from CBS Sports make the Dodgers the favorites to win it all, with Milwaukee next. For Toronto, the season ends without a trip to October. The Blue Jays finished seventy-nine and eighty-three, missing the field despite MC's hometown loyalty. A five hundred record still won a division. That's how uneven this season was.

Story two. Three weeks in, parity is the story.

Through three weeks, four teams are still unbeaten: San Francisco, Minnesota, Kansas City and, most surprisingly, Las Vegas, whose three and oh start under new coach Klint Kubiak is already drawing early coach of the year talk. San Francisco beat Arizona thirty-six to thirty despite missing stars Nick Bosa and Trent Williams to injury, and Minnesota moved to three and oh by beating Tampa Bay twenty-three to sixteen. Kansas City stayed unbeaten too, handling Miami twenty-four to ten behind two touchdown passes from Patrick Mahomes. In a tighter AFC South game, Indianapolis edged Houston nineteen to seventeen.

Sunday also delivered two of the year's best finishes so far. In Washington, defending Super Bowl champion Seattle lost thirty-three to thirty-one to the Commanders, who won it with backup quarterback Marcus Mariota running the offense. In an international game played in Brazil, Baltimore beat Dallas thirty-four to thirty-one on a walk-off field goal from Tyler Loop as time expired. Pittsburgh also survived a scare, holding off Cincinnati thirty to twenty-seven after Derrick Harmon stripped Joe Burrow late and Sebastian Joseph-Day recovered the fumble to seal it. And New England's rough day continued: quarterback Drake Maye turned the ball over seven times in a thirty-five to six loss to Jacksonville.

Here's why it matters. Three games in, the league's best records belong to teams that weren't expected to be there, especially Las Vegas, while a defending champion has already lost twice. It's early, but this season is not shaping up the way most preseason predictions had it.

Story three. Golf's biggest final-day comeback, ever.

At Medinah Country Club outside Chicago, the United States completed the largest final-day comeback in Presidents Cup history, turning a three point deficit into a seventeen to thirteen win over the International team. The Americans trailed ten and a half to seven and a half heading into Sunday's singles matches, then took nine and a half of the twelve points available to close it out. It's the U.S. team's eleventh straight win in the event and its fourteenth overall.

Jacob Bridgeman clinched the cup outright with a one up win over Min Woo Lee on the eighteenth hole. Cameron Young beat Ryo Hisatsune three and two, capped by a thirty-one foot birdie putt, and Patrick Cantlay beat Adam Scott two and one with a fifty-seven foot birdie of his own on the sixteenth. Scottie Scheffler closed out Nico Echavarria two and one, and drew attention for a different reason. After a fan yelled during Echavarria's backswing, Scheffler confronted the spectator, saying, quote: there's no place for that, end quote, then conceded his opponent's next putt rather than let the disruption affect the hole. The International team's only Sunday point came from Tom Kim, who beat Wyndham Clark three and two.

Here's why it matters. Before Sunday, the Internationals had never even led this event on American soil at any point in its history, and their only outright win in the competition's history came back in nineteen ninety eight, with one tie in two thousand three. Sunday's rally keeps the Americans' streak alive, but the format nearly flipped for the first time in decades on U.S. soil, which is the closest this event has come to a real contest in a very long time.

And finally, one thread ran through all three results today. Being good all year guaranteed nothing by the end of it. A five hundred baseball team is playing in October, a defending Super Bowl champion has already lost twice, and the best golfers on the planet nearly gave up an eleven year winning streak in a single afternoon.
