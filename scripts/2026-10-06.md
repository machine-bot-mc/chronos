## Full script
Good morning. It's Tuesday, October sixth, and this is your Chronos digest. Today: business.

Three Canadian-flavoured stories from the past week. An oil sands takeover from Cenovus. A holdup in China for the mining merger of Anglo American and Teck. And Rogers taking full ownership of the company behind the Leafs, the Raptors and more.

Story one. Cenovus buys Athabasca Oil.

On Monday, October fifth, Cenovus Energy announced a definitive agreement to acquire Athabasca Oil Corporation. According to the Cenovus news release, the offer is worth twelve dollars per Athabasca share, paid in a mix of cash and stock. The deal carries an implied enterprise value of five point seven billion dollars.

Here are the specifics. Between sixty-five and seventy-five percent of the payment is cash, up to four point three billion dollars. The rest is Cenovus stock, up to about forty-four million shares. Athabasca shareholders who prefer stock would receive point two six four Cenovus shares for each share they hold.

What does Cenovus get? About forty-five thousand barrels of oil equivalent a day of production. That includes thermal oil sands production next to Cenovus's existing Christina Lake, May River and Thornbury assets. The release also cites more than seventy-five years of proved plus probable reserve life, and a potential to grow Athabasca's Duvernay shale platform to twenty thousand barrels a day. Cenovus expects eighty-five million dollars a year in corporate and commercial savings.

The caveats. The deal still needs regulatory approvals and a vote by Athabasca shareholders. Both boards approved it unanimously, and directors and officers holding about two point two percent of the shares have agreed to vote in favour. Closing is expected in December. There is no financing condition.

Why it matters. Cenovus says its net debt is about three billion dollars now, and would rise to between five and five and a half billion dollars by the end of this year, assuming current futures prices. Its long-term target is four billion dollars, unchanged. So this is a big bet on oil sands, funded in part by borrowing. CEO Jon McKenzie called it, quote: "a natural extension of our oil sands strategy." End quote.

Story two. China holds the key to Anglo Teck.

Reuters reported on October second that China's market regulator, known as SAMR, is asking Anglo American for commitments on copper supply before it will approve the merger with Teck. Reuters cites three unnamed sources familiar with the confidential talks. The reporters are Clara Denina and Divya Rajagopal.

According to that report, China wants Anglo to commit to steady supplies of copper concentrate, including volumes that Anglo sells through traders. Anglo's copper comes mainly from Peru and Chile and is sold as unrefined concentrate, which Chinese smelters buy in large amounts.

The report says every other regulator in the countries where the companies operate has already approved the deal. It values the combined transaction at fifty-four billion US dollars. The combined company would control roughly five percent of global copper supply, which is below the ten to fifteen percent levels the report mentions as competition thresholds. No asset sales are currently being sought.

Anglo American told Reuters, quote: "We are making good progress towards completion and are working constructively with the Chinese regulator." End quote. Teck declined to comment. Both companies expect to finish by March twenty twenty-seven.

Why it matters. Teck is one of Canada's biggest mining companies. The report shows a single regulator, in a single country, can still set the pace on a deal this size. And the sources are unnamed, so treat the details as reported, not confirmed.

Story three. Rogers owns all of MLSE.

On October first, Rogers Communications announced it had completed the purchase of the remaining twenty-five percent of Maple Leaf Sports and Entertainment from Kilmer Sports. The price was four point three five billion Canadian dollars. Rogers now owns all of MLSE.

Rogers will create a new business unit called Rogers Sports. It will bring together MLSE, the Toronto Blue Jays and Rogers's other sports, media and entertainment operations. Keith Pelley stays on as MLSE president and CEO, with added responsibility for Rogers Media. Mark Shapiro stays as president and CEO of the Blue Jays. The company says it will work out an operating structure for the new unit.

CEO Tony Staffieri said, quote: "We're proud to bring these beloved teams together with the rest of Rogers to deliver more for fans, audiences, and customers." End quote.

What the release does not say is what the deal implies for the value of the whole company. It also gives no detail on what changes for fans or for broadcast rights. Those are open questions.

Why it matters. Rogers is now tying its phone, internet and media business directly to the teams. For a Toronto sports fan, one company now controls more of what you watch and where you watch it.

And finally, one thing worth trying. If you advise founders, all three stories turn on the same question: who controls the pipes, the supply, or the audience. Pick one client's business and ask who holds that lever.

That's your digest. It's Tuesday, October sixth. Have a good one.
