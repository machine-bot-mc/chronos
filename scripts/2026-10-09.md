## Full script
Good morning. It's Friday, October ninth, and this is your Chronos digest. Today: international news.

Three stories from the past few days. Fighting in Ethiopia's Tigray region, and who might get pulled in. The Iran war's spillover, from Riyadh's airport to the Strait of Hormuz. And a shooting by an immigration agent in New York.

Story one. Ethiopia's Tigray war.

Al Jazeera reported on October ninth that Ethiopian federal forces have entered Mekelle, the capital of the Tigray region, after nearly two weeks of fighting. Forces from a rebel alliance that includes the Tigray People's Liberation Front withdrew from the city. Al Jazeera says the fighting began in late September and has effectively ended the twenty twenty-two Pretoria ceasefire.

Federal forces used air power and ground troops. The rebels lost ground around the towns of Alamata and Kobo, then pulled back into the countryside. Fighting continues elsewhere in Tigray.

The Associated Press, as cited by Al Jazeera, reported Eritrean troops entering the towns of Adigrat and Idaga Hamus. Eritrea has neither confirmed nor denied that. Eritrea also denies supporting the rebel alliance, and Egypt and Sudan deny Ethiopian accusations that they back it.

On October first, Ethiopia closed its embassy in Eritrea and expelled Eritrean diplomats. Eritrea then cut diplomatic ties. On October fourth, Egypt, Eritrea, Sudan and Somalia held a summit in New Alamein, Egypt, and called for respect for sovereignty and territorial integrity. Al Jazeera notes that is not a formal military alliance.

Behind this is Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed's push for sea access. Ethiopia has been landlocked since Eritrea became independent in nineteen ninety-one. Abiy told parliament, quote: "Based on the principle of give and take, we will work diligently to ensure that Ethiopia has an outlet to the sea." End quote. The Tigrayan leader, Debretsion Gebremichael, said, quote: "We will confront the enemy on our terms and where we choose." End quote.

Why it matters. Analysts quoted by Al Jazeera warn that Egypt and Eritrea see Ethiopia's Red Sea ambitions as a strategic threat. Whether the rivals stay diplomatic or move onto the battlefield is, for now, an open question. The article also notes that when federal forces took Mekelle in the twenty twenty war, Tigrayan forces later regrouped and retook it. Taking a capital has not ended this conflict before.

Story two. The Iran war spills over.

Al Jazeera reported on October ninth that Saudi Arabia's Civil Aviation Authority said King Khalid International Airport, north of Riyadh, was hit by two attacks on Thursday. Three Saudi citizens were killed. The page I read does not say who carried out the attacks, so I won't guess.

In the Strait of Hormuz, a political deputy of the Iranian Revolutionary Guard navy said, quote: "Iranian forces have been carrying out operations every night in the Strait of Hormuz." End quote. He said they target vessels Tehran considers to be breaking its rules. That is Iran's description. I could not independently confirm it.

Al Jazeera also reported that the aircraft carrier USS Abraham Lincoln returned to San Diego on Thursday after three hundred twenty-two days deployed, including a record two hundred sixty-five consecutive days at sea, supporting the US and Israeli war on Iran, which has continued since February. Reports described food shortages and poor conditions. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth accused media of trying to portray the ship as a symbol of, quote: "a failed mission." End quote. President Trump called the reporting fake news.

On public opinion, Al Jazeera cites the Pew Research Center: sixty-five percent of Americans disapprove of Trump's handling of the war, and thirty-three percent approve.

Why it matters. The strait carries fuel, fertilizer and other goods, so disruption there reaches prices far from the Gulf. For you in Toronto, that means energy and shipping costs are worth watching, though none of these sources give a Canadian figure.

Story three. A shooting in New York.

On Thursday afternoon, an immigration agent shot a man sitting in a car with a five-year-old child, in the Marble Hill area of New York, according to Al Jazeera on October ninth. Police identified the man as Oscar Belgal, twenty-eight. He was shot below the left ear, was conscious, and was being treated at a hospital. The child was unharmed.

The accounts differ. The Department of Homeland Security says officers were conducting a targeted enforcement operation, describes Belgal as an arrest target with a criminal conviction, and says an officer discharged his weapon. Police Commissioner Jessica Tisch said Belgal was pulling into a parking space when a plain-clothes federal officer approached with a gun drawn. She said he began to move the car forward, and at least one officer fired seven rounds.

Mayor Zohran Mamdani called it, quote: "unconscionable and incredibly serious." End quote. He said ICE must be abolished. Governor Kathy Hochul urged a halt to ICE operations in New York and asked that body camera footage be released. Protesters marched to the hospital, and there were scuffles with police.

Why it matters. The incident is under investigation, and the key question, what the officers saw when they fired, is not yet answered publicly. Body camera footage could settle some of it.

And finally, a note on sourcing. All three stories today come from Al Jazeera's pages. I could not open other outlets this morning, so treat these as one outlet's account, and watch for wire coverage to confirm the details.

That's your digest. It's Friday, October ninth. Have a good one.
