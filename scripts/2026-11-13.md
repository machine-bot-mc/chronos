## Full script
Good morning. It's Friday, November thirteenth, and this is your Chronos digest. Today: international news.

Three stories. Russia and Ukraine meet face to face for the first time in nine months, and agree on a large prisoner swap. Iran says more tankers have been hit by mines in the Strait of Hormuz, as both sides harden their positions. And Indonesia's vice president resigns over a dispute about his school diploma.

Story one. A prisoner swap, but no breakthrough.

The Associated Press reported on Wednesday, November eleventh, that Russian and Ukrainian delegations held two days of talks in Abu Dhabi, mediated by the United States. It was their first direct meeting in nine months. Russia's team was led by Vladimir Medinsky, Ukraine's by Rustem Umerov. Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff represented Washington.

The concrete result was a deal, in principle, on a large exchange of prisoners of war. The final numbers had not been announced. A Russian officer said the swap could happen within two or three weeks. Kushner called it, quote: "the biggest humanitarian release of prisoners." End quote. He also said, quote: "The reality is, is there's no trust right now between the sides." End quote.

The big questions remain open. They include security guarantees after the war, the status of Russian-occupied land, and what role Europe and NATO should play. President Zelenskyy said Ukraine will not give up occupied territory.

On Thursday, the AP reported that Kushner and Witkoff met President Putin in the Kremlin for about two hours, then flew to Dublin to see Zelenskyy, who was meeting leaders from more than forty European countries. Putin said European troops in Ukraine after a ceasefire would be, quote: "absolutely unacceptable." End quote. He said Russia would treat them as legitimate targets. NATO's Secretary-General Mark Rutte said, quote: "we need the peace deal, or at least a ceasefire." End quote.

The fighting continues. Ukraine's air force said Russia launched missiles and one hundred and twenty-three drones overnight into Thursday. Russia said it shot down seventy-one Ukrainian drones. Those figures come from the two sides and were not independently verified.

Here's why it matters. A big prisoner swap is a real, human result. But the gap on Europe's role, and on territory, is as wide as ever.

Story two. Mines and hard words in the Strait of Hormuz.

Reuters reported from Dubai on Thursday, November twelfth, that Iran's military says four oil tankers were hit by explosions in the Strait of Hormuz. Iran's Revolutionary Guards said two supertankers were disabled on Wednesday after ignoring warnings against what it called an "unauthorised route." Iran's army said two more tankers hit mines on Thursday afternoon. Reuters said it could not verify the Iranian account.

Separately, the UK Maritime Trade Operations agency said a vessel was hit by an unidentified projectile off the United Arab Emirates on Wednesday, causing an engine-room fire. All crew were evacuated. CBS News reported that, early Friday, the Revolutionary Guards said a ship carrying liquefied natural gas had also struck a mine. CBS notes the US military says it has cleared the strait's routes of mines.

The scale is large. Reuters cited the Washington Institute for Near East Policy, which counts at least ten ships hit in the first ten days of November, after thirty-seven attacks in October.

Diplomacy looks stuck. According to CBS News, President Trump posted on Thursday, quote: "We don't need a deal with Iran." End quote. Of a peace deal, he wrote, quote: "Those days have long since passed." End quote. On the Iranian side, the head of the Basij militia said Tehran will discuss the terms of any talks only after its conditions are met. Those conditions include easing sanctions, lifting the US naval blockade, releasing frozen assets, and an Iran and Oman system for controlling traffic in the strait. Secretary of State Marco Rubio has called the demands unacceptable.

Here's why it matters. Reuters notes that tankers are still being hit even as traffic rebounds and oil prices fall on hopes of calm. That gap between market hope and events at sea is the thing to watch.

Story three. Indonesia's vice president steps down.

The Jakarta Post reported that on Thursday, November twelfth, Vice President Gibran Rakabuming Raka resigned. He is thirty-nine, and the son of former president Joko Widodo, known as Jokowi.

The trigger was his education. The Constitutional Court and the Supreme Court separately ruled last month that his overseas schooling did not meet the requirements to run in the twenty twenty-four election. The Post reported on Wednesday that one hundred and twenty lawmakers had signed a petition that could lead to his impeachment.

Gibran handed a handwritten letter to the leaders of the People's Consultative Assembly. According to the assembly's speaker, he wrote that he was resigning, quote: "through my own volition ... for the interests of the government and nation." End quote. In a video that evening, Gibran said the decision had nothing to do with the controversy over his credentials.

Jokowi said, quote: "We will continue to support President Prabowo's administration." End quote. Under the process described by Indonesia's law minister, the assembly declares a vacancy, the president proposes two candidates, and a vote follows within sixty days. Reuters, in a report carried by the Post, said the rupiah fell to a record low against the US dollar that day.

Here's why it matters. Indonesia is Southeast Asia's largest economy, and Reuters reports the government already faces scandals and doubts about its management. The Post wrote that this resignation effectively ends the alliance between Prabowo and Jokowi. Analysts quoted by Reuters said Jokowi's pledge of support may ease the strain.

That's your digest. It's Friday, November thirteenth. Have a good one.
